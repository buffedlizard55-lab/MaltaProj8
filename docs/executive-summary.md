# Executive Summary — Healthcare with 0 Earned Income in California After H.R.1

**Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Jurisdiction:** United States / California · **Subject:** How a U.S. Citizen and Permanent California Resident, Full-Time Property Manager and Kalshi Trader with Income Only from Kalshi Futures Contracts, Rental Income, and Savings Interest (Zero Earned Income as Traditionally Understood) Maintains Healthcare Coverage Under the New Federal Medicaid Work Requirements (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, P.L. 119-21).

---

## The Short Answer

**You do NOT need earned income to keep healthcare in California.**

1.  **The Medicaid work requirement is misnamed.** Federal law calls it a "community engagement requirement." Only **one** of **seven** qualifying paths must be met, and the **easiest path — earning $580/month from *any* source — counts unearned income** (rental net + Kalshi gains + bank interest). You do not need a W-2 job, and you do not need to log 80 hours of work if your passive/investment income meets the threshold.
2.  **Your income profile (rental + Kalshi + interest) *is* your compliance mechanism.** California DHCS guidance (ACWDL 25-30, Dec 30, 2025) explicitly lists — as countable income toward the $580 test — *unemployment benefits, State Disability Insurance, retirement income, dividends,* and the same chart that DHCS published for MAGI counting treats *net rental income, interest (taxable and tax-exempt), and capital gains* as countable. All three of your income streams count.
3.  **If your MAGI is low, you are on Medi-Cal (free). If higher, you are on Covered California with subsidies. Neither marketplace requires earned income.** Covered California asks for MAGI, not earned income, per [InsureMeKevin / DHCS MAGI chart](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MAGI-INCOME-AND-DEDUCTION-TYPES.pdf) and [Covered California — What Counts as Income](https://www.coveredca.com/support/financial-help/what-counts-as-income/).
4.  **California is actively reducing the burden:** ex parte verification first, 30-day cure period, automatic hardship exemptions for disaster/high-unemployment counties, outreach 3 months before renewal, and lead-plaintiff in a June 2026 multi-state lawsuit challenging the rule.
5.  **Critical IRS warning on Kalshi:** There is **no IRS Revenue Ruling, Regulation, or Notice** on Kalshi as of October 2026. The classification — capital gains vs. gambling vs. §1256 — is unsettled and determines how much of your Kalshi profit counts, how losses offset, and whether you face the new 90% gambling-loss cap from 2026 (OBBBA §70114). For MAGI, capital-gains treatment (net gains on Schedule D) is *most favorable*; gambling treatment (gross winnings on Schedule 1) is *least favorable* and creates phantom income.

## The New Law — Exact Details (No Paraphrase Without Source)

- **Statute:** H.R.1, 119th Congress, signed July 4, 2025 as **Public Law 119-21, Section 71119**.
- **Full text:** [congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/text) — search "Sec. 71119" and "Sec. 71107".
- **CMS rule:** Interim Final Rule with Comment Period **CMS-2454-IFC**, issued June 1, 2026, published June 3, 2026 at [91 FR 33348](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/03/2026-11094/medicaid-program-community-engagement-requirement-for-certain-individuals), effective July 31, 2026. Fact sheet: [cms.gov](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals-interim-final-rule-comment-period-cms).
- **Who it applies to:** Non-pregnant, non-disabled adults **19-64** eligible under the ACA expansion pathway ([§1902(a)(10)(A)(i)(VIII) of the Social Security Act](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48633)). In California = **MAGI New Adult Group**, Aid Code M1. [DHCS ACWDL 25-30, Dec 30, 2025](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/25-30.pdf).
- **Deadline:** States must implement by **January 1, 2027** (first quarter after Dec 31, 2026). Good-faith delay to **Dec 31, 2028** possible at HHS Secretary discretion. [Ibid. and KFF tracker](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview/).
- **Renewals:** M1 group renewals move from **every 12 months → every 6 months** starting Jan 2027 per **Sec. 71107**. [DHCS MEDIL I26-25](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/MEDIL-I26-25.pdf). Exempt individuals stay annual.
- **Requirement (verbatim from statute):** Individual demonstrates community engagement for a month if they meet *one or more* of:
  > (A) work ≥80 hrs; (B) community service ≥80 hrs; (C) work program ≥80 hrs; (D) enrolled at least half-time; (E) combination ≥80 hrs; **(F) monthly income ≥ federal minimum wage × 80 hrs (currently $7.25 × 80 = $580)**; (G) seasonal workers — average monthly income over prior 6 months ≥ $580.
- **Verification:** State must verify at application (look-back **1-3 consecutive months** immediately before application month, state option) and at renewal (**≥1 month** between redeterminations). Must use **ex parte data-matching where possible** before asking enrollee. If noncompliant, must issue **notice of noncompliance** and give **30 days to verify**. [CMS IFC and KFF summary](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/a-closer-look-at-the-work-requirement-provisions-in-the-2025-federal-budget-reconciliation-law/).

## Exemptions — Who Does NOT Have to Meet It

Per statute and IFC fact sheet, **mandatory** exemptions (states *must* exempt):

- Parents/guardians/caregivers of a **dependent child ≤13** or a **disabled individual** (any age)
- **Veterans with a total disability rating**
- **Medically frail / special medical needs** — blind, disabled, substance-use disorder, mental disorder, significant physical/intellectual/developmental disability, serious & complex medical condition
- Pregnant individuals and **12-month postpartum** coverage period
- **American Indians / Alaska Natives** (IHCIA definition)
- **Former foster care youth** (under 26, in foster care at 18+), **foster youth**
- Aged/Blind/Disabled eligibility-group members (including SSI recipients)
- Individuals **entitled to/enrolled in Medicare Part A or B**
- Those **complying with TANF work requirements** or **SNAP recipients not exempt from SNAP work**
- **Inmates of a public institution**, and those **released within the past 90 days**
- Participants in **drug/alcohol treatment program**

*Plus* **optional hardship** (state-elective; **California has elected to implement ALL** — see below).

Full list: [CMS Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals-interim-final-rule-comment-period-cms) and [DHCS Implementation Plan](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/federal-impacts/Documents/DHCS-HR1-Implementation-Plan.pdf).

## California — What Is the State Doing to Help Those with 0 Earned Income?

California cannot exempt the expansion group from the federal requirement, but DHCS is minimizing disruption in at least seven ways — all verified:

### 1. Unearned income counts toward the $580 threshold
DHCS ACWDL 25-30, page 7 table: qualifying income includes *"Individual's total gross income for a month must meet or exceed this amount"* and the same guidance letter lists **total gross income** with examples covering *unemployment benefits, State Disability Insurance, retirement income, dividends, etc.* The DHCS MAGI Income Chart for Medi-Cal counts as **Countable: Interest (taxable and non-taxable), Rental real estate (Schedule E), Capital gain/loss (Schedule D)**. No mention of an "earned income" restriction.

→ Your **savings interest + net rental + Kalshi capital gains** all count. If you have $800 rental net and $100 interest in any month, you already satisfy the requirement that month.

### 2. Six-month averaging for seasonal/irregular income
If your Kalshi trading is seasonal or lumpy, California must allow you to **average monthly income over the prior 6 months** to meet $580. This protects traders who have a zero-income month between profitable months.

### 3. All optional hardship exemptions adopted
Per DHCS HR1 Implementation Plan (Jan 29, 2026): California will **automatically apply** — based on residence — hardship exemptions for:
- Living in a county with a **federally declared emergency/disaster**
- Living in a county/jurisdiction with **high unemployment** (threshold TBD by CMS/state)
And **by request**:
- **Inpatient care** (hospital, SNF, nursing facility) and related **outpatient care**
- **Medical travel** for complex conditions unavailable locally

During those months the work requirement is waived entirely.

### 4. Ex parte verification first
Counties must check existing state data (EDD wage data, FTB income, UI, program participation) **before** asking you for paperwork. Only if the state cannot verify do you get a notice. You then have **30 days** to respond, and **retain coverage during the 30 days**.

### 5. Outreach 3 months before your first look-back
DHCS MEDIL I26-25 template letter pledges: you will receive notice **at least 3 months before** the first compliance look-back period, and periodically thereafter, explaining how to comply, exemptions, consequences, and how to report.

### 6. Lead plaintiff in federal lawsuit
California is lead plaintiff in **Commonwealth of Massachusetts et al. v. Mehmet Oz, M.D. et al.** (June 2026), a multi-state challenge to the IFC. [DHCS Tracking page](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/tracking-federal-impact-medi-cal-eligibility/). Outcome pending — could delay or modify requirements.

### 7. Medi-Cal remains an option even if you briefly exceed income limit
If your MAGI in a given 6-month period exceeds 138% FPL, you are not penalized: you transition to **Covered California with subsidies** (no work requirement, rolling enrollment via special enrollment period). You can re-enroll in Medi-Cal when income drops — no lockout.

> **Cost of implementation:** DHCS estimates **$1.1B General Fund in FY2026-27** and **$9.6B in lost federal revenue by FY2029-30** due to disenrollment — cited in [CalHHS Connect webinar Feb 2026](https://www.chhs.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CALHHS-CONNECT-Webinar-2.pdf).

## Detailed Recommendation for the Profile (0 Earned Income; Rental + Kalshi + Interest)

### Primary strategy: Pass the Income Test (no hours, no volunteering)

1. **Keep your combined countable income ≥ $580 in at least one month per 6-month period.** Because California verifies *at least one month between redeterminations* (DHCS will likely require one qualifying month since last renewal — per MEDIL I26-25: "at least $580... in any month since your last renewal"), you do **not** need $580 *every* month — just **one qualifying month** in the window.
2. **Document sources you already have:**
   - **Rental:** Schedule E + lease + bank deposits (net amount after expenses)
   - **Interest:** 1099-INT + bank statements
   - **Kalshi:** P&L statement (FIFO) from Kalshi + your Schedule D/Form 8949. Note: Kalshi does NOT issue a 1099 for trading gains as of 2026 (only 1099-INT for interest, 1099-MISC for rewards). You must self-report.
3. **If one income type alone is < $580, sum them.** The threshold is **total gross income from *all* countable sources in the month.** $300 interest + $350 rental = $650 → PASS.
4. **If all three are zero in a month, rely on the 6-month average rule:** average over prior 6 months ≥ $580 qualifies.
5. **At renewal, let ex parte do its work.** State checks FTB/EDD first. Respond within 30 days only if you receive a notice.

### Fallback strategies (if income dips to zero for 6+ months — rare with rental):

- **Volunteer 80 hrs/month** (20 hrs/week) at a qualifying organization. Keep timesheets — state will publish qualifying org list.
- **Enroll half-time in college or career/technical training** — even one qualifying course per term at half-time status suffices.
- **Reclassify property management as a business:** If you provide active management services (leasing, showings, maintenance coordination, bookkeeping) for yourself or others, file a **Schedule C** (self-employment) and log **80 hrs/month** of management activity. This creates both "work hours" and earned income. **Note:** Pure passive ownership (collecting rent on Schedule E with no active services) does NOT generate work hours — only income-test dollars.

### What NOT to do

- **Do not intentionally reduce rental/interest income to stay under *lower* thresholds** — there is no minimum-income requirement for Medi-Cal; lower income *helps* eligibility. The $580 test is a *maximum* you *want* to exceed, not a limit you stay below.
- **Do not misclassify Kalshi as gambling to reduce MAGI.** Gambling losses on Schedule A do NOT reduce AGI/MAGI, so they increase your MAGI (exposing you to subsidy cliff) and trigger the 90% cap. Capital-gain treatment (Schedule D) lets losses offset gains and reduce MAGI, but IRS has NOT blessed any Kalshi classification — consult a CPA.
- **Do not ignore county mail.** An estimated 60-70% of coverage losses nationally are projected to be **procedural** (people remain income-eligible but miss the 30-day cure window). [CBO, KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/a-closer-look-at-the-work-requirement-provisions-in-the-2025-federal-budget-reconciliation-law/).

## What California Is NOT Doing (And Why)

California is **not** opting out of the work requirement — it **cannot**. Federal law (H.R.1 §71119) makes the requirement mandatory for expansion states; only the Secretary may grant a good-faith delay to Dec 31, 2028, and no waiver under §1115 is allowed. California is seeking through the multi-state lawsuit to narrow or enjoin the IFC, but has simultaneously begun full implementation planning to meet the Jan 1, 2027 deadline.
