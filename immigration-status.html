Changes coming between 2026 and 2027

ItemValueDetailsSource
January 1, 2026 — adult enrollment freeze (active now) Some adults can no longer sign up for full-scope Medi-Cal because of immigration status

If you already have full-scope Medi-Cal you can keep it regardless of status, as long as you renew on time and still meet Medi-Cal rules. If your Medi-Cal ends you have three months to sign up again; if you miss that, you can only get restricted-scope Medi-Cal (emergency care, pregnancy care, nursing home care).

 Medi-Cal Changes (2026–2027 change calendar) DHCS
October 2026 — federal reclassification of certain statuses State-funded full-scope Medi-Cal until June 30, 2027 for affected people

Affected groups listed by DHCS: refugees and asylees, humanitarian parolees, and survivors of domestic violence or human trafficking with a pending case. From July 1, 2027 these groups "will only be able to get pregnancy-related care and emergency services." Permanent residents are not in this group.

 Medi-Cal Changes (2026–2027 change calendar) DHCS
January 1, 2027 — Fee-for-Service transition Some members move from a health plan to fee-for-service

Applies to people who are undocumented, some green card holders in the five-year waiting period, and people considered Permanently Residing in the U.S. Under Color of Law. "Fee-for-Service does not mean you pay. Medi-Cal still pays for your care." Enhanced Care Management and Community Supports are plan-only services and do not continue in FFS.

 Medi-Cal Changes (2026–2027 change calendar) DHCS
July 1, 2027 — dental coverage and monthly premiums Some members lose full dental or must pay a monthly premium

If you do not pay the premium, "your Medi-Cal will change to restricted-scope, which covers: Emergency care; Pregnancy-related care; Nursing home care." Children, pregnant people, and former foster youth under 26 are exempt from the premium and keep full dental.

 Medi-Cal Changes (2026–2027 change calendar) DHCS
October 1, 2026 — section 71109 federal funding rule Federal Medicaid money may only cover citizens, permanent residents, Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA residents

Section 71109 bars federal Medicaid funding for people who are residents of the U.S. but fall outside those four categories, and extends the bar to CHIP. It also ends federal Medicaid funding for refugees and asylees (who can qualify as permanent residents after one year). A lawful permanent resident is inside the categories that keep funding, so this provision does not cut you off; it is the federal rule behind the state-funded full-scope coverage California offers certain other groups until June 30, 2027.

How immigration status interacts with the work rule

ItemValueDetailsSource
California's rule The work rule applies "regardless of immigration status" to the MAGI New Adult Group

DHCS ACWDL 25-30, page 1. California law codifies this: WIC § 14005.69(b)(1)(B) includes state-funded Medi-Cal adults who meet the same criteria as the ACA adult group.

 ACWDL 25-30 — Work and Community Engagement Requirements for New Adult Group DHCS
Why it matters to you Your status does not exempt you; the $580 income test and the 80-hour options are the same for everyone

The rule operates on the coverage group and the individual's circumstances, not on immigration status.
