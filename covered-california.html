Federal premium tax credits, the California Premium Subsidy, what you would actually pay, and the 2027 open-enrollment dates.

Medi-Cal or Covered California?

ItemValueDetailsSource
If your household income is at or below 138% FPL Medi-Cal

Covered California: "Californians with incomes at or below 138% of the federal poverty level" are eligible for Medi-Cal, and it is free or low cost.

 Medi-Cal (program overview and eligibility) Covered California
If you need insurance and are not eligible for Medi-Cal Covered California

Covered California is "a private health insurance marketplace where people can get income-based financial help to reduce monthly premiums."

 Medi-Cal (program overview and eligibility) Covered California
If your income is above 138% FPL Covered California with financial help (if under 400% FPL)

The federal premium tax credit covers 100%–400% FPL; the California Premium Subsidy adds state help up to 200% FPL in 2027.

If you lose Medi-Cal for work-rule non-compliance Covered California with no financial help

DHCS member notice: "you cannot get financial help to pay for a health plan through Covered California. That means you would have to pay the full cost of new health insurance." The county policy letter says the same: individuals who fail the work rule "will not be eligible for subsidized coverage through Covered California, even if they meet all other Medi-Cal requirements."

Federal premium tax credit rules

ItemValueDetailsSource
Income floor Household income must be at least 100% of the FPL

IRS: "Your household income is at least 100 percent* but not more than 400 percent of the federal poverty line (FPL) for your family size." The IRS adds a footnote: "Under special circumstances, you may be able to claim the PTC even though your income is below 100 percent of the federal poverty line. See the instructions for Form 8962." For 2026, 100% FPL for one person is $15,960.

 Premium Tax Credit (PTC) overview Internal Revenue Service (IRS)
Annual Update of the HHS Poverty Guidelines (91 FR 1797) Federal Register / HHS
Income ceiling Household income must not be more than 400% of the FPL

The temporary elimination of the 400% cap applied only to tax years 2021–2025, so the cap is back for 2026 and 2027. If income exceeds 400% FPL you are not allowed a premium tax credit and must repay advance payments.

 Premium Tax Credit (PTC) overview Internal Revenue Service (IRS)
Other conditions Not eligible for Medicaid/Medicare/CHIP, no affordable employer plan, all premiums paid by the tax due date

Also: not claimed as a dependent by another person; Married Filing Separately only with an exception; must file a return with Form 8962 if you received advance payments.

What happens if you are below 100% FPL in California Medi-Cal, not a subsidy

Because California has expanded Medicaid, the 100% floor is not a coverage gap here — it is the line below which you are routed to Medi-Cal instead of a marketplace plan.

The California Premium Subsidy (state money)

This is the part of the 2027 answer that is purely California. It applies on top of, not instead of, the federal credit.

ItemValueDetailsSource
2026 program Up to 165% FPL, $190 million

The 2026 California Premium Subsidy Program was available to enrollees at or below 165% FPL who were also eligible for the federal advanced premium tax credit and applied on the subsidized application. It preserved IRA-level premium subsidies for enrollees up to 150% FPL.

 Financial Help Basics for Enrollers Quick Guide (2026 California Premium Subsidy, 165% FPL) Covered California (enroller toolkit)
2027 program Up to 200% FPL, $300 million

Covered California, July 21, 2026: the Legislature appropriated $300 million from the Health Care Affordability Reserve Fund, up from $190 million. "More than 500,000 Californians are projected to receive a state subsidy in 2027, about 30 percent of all enrollees," and nearly 200,000 can choose from two Silver-tier plans with a $0 premium.

 Covered California Rates and Plans for 2027 (news release) Covered California
How the 2027 design works Preserves $0-premium Silver plans up to 150% FPL and lowers premiums from 150% to 200% FPL

The 2027 program design "preserves IRA-level premium subsidies for the lowest-income Covered California enrollees up to 150 percent FPL and lowers federally-required premiums for individuals between 150 and 200 percent FPL." Eligibility for the state subsidy requires eligibility for the federal APTC and applying on the subsidized application.

 2027 California State Premium Subsidy Program Design Covered California Board (hbex.coveredca.com / board.coveredca.com)
Does it help if you have $0 income? No — you use Medi-Cal instead

The state subsidy supplements federal APTC, and APTC requires household income of at least 100% FPL. Below that, Medi-Cal is the program (and in California it is free or low cost).

2027 change to financial help for immigrant households

ItemValueDetailsSource
Groups that keep financial help in 2027 Lawful permanent residents (green card holders), Cuban and Haitian entrants, Compact of Free Association migrants

Covered California: "Starting in 2027, only certain groups — such as green card holders — will be eligible for financial help to lower their monthly costs."

 Information for Immigrants Covered California
Groups that lose financial help in 2027 Asylum grantees, refugees, trafficking survivors (T visa), Temporary Protected Status, work visa holders, student visa holders

Covered California lists these as the major immigrant groups who will no longer receive financial help. This follows H.R. 1. Covered California also notes: "If you have health insurance through Covered California now, you can keep your financial help in 2026."

 Information for Immigrants Covered California
Federal Changes for Enrollers Quick Guide (H.R. 1 changes, 2027 financial help) Covered California (enroller toolkit)
What this means for a green card holder You keep marketplace financial help in 2027

This is a change in the law, not a change in your eligibility. Covered California: "You can still keep your health insurance through Covered California in 2027" — the cost changes for some groups, not for permanent residents.

 Information for Immigrants Covered California
Applying does not put your immigration status at risk "Applying will not affect your immigration status or your chances of becoming a lawful permanent resident or U.S. citizen."

Covered California also advises listing everyone in the household regardless of status, because household size and income drive eligibility for the whole family.

 Information for Immigrants Covered California
Public charge note (if you are still seeking a green card or citizenship) flagged not confirmed

Covered California states that a federal rule finalised beginning September 2026 changes how public charge is determined, that getting financial help through Covered California may affect a green card application, and that "Most consumers who are eligible for financial help in 2027 may not be subject to the public charge rule when they apply for admission or a green card," with exceptions for asylum, refugee, U visa, T visa, VAWA and Special Immigrant Juvenile Status applicants.
