Assets: who is subject, who is not

California reinstated asset limits for non-MAGI programs on January 1, 2026. Adults in the MAGI New Adult Group are not subject to an asset test.

ItemValueDetailsSource
Who is subject to an asset test People 65 or older, people with a disability (physical, mental, or developmental), people in a nursing home, and families whose income is too high to qualify under federal tax rules

DHCS's asset-limit FAQ lists exactly these four groups. "Medi-Cal started counting your assets (things you own) on January 1, 2026, to decide if you can get or keep coverage."

 Asset Limit Frequently Asked Questions DHCS
Implementation Plan for New Federal Eligibility and Enrollment Changes under H.R. 1 DHCS
Who is not subject Adults 19–64 in the MAGI New Adult Group (and other MAGI-based groups)

The asset limits apply to non-MAGI programs; MAGI-based eligibility is determined on income alone. If you are 19–64 and eligible as a Medi-Cal adult, your savings and rental property are not counted against your Medi-Cal eligibility.

 Asset Limit Frequently Asked Questions DHCS
Asset limit through June 30, 2027 $130,000 for one person, plus $65,000 for each additional household member (up to 10)

DHCS asset-limit FAQ. Countable: bank accounts, cash, second vehicles, second homes, other financial resources. Not counted: your main home, your main vehicle, household items, and retirement funds if you are taking regular payments.

 Asset Limit Frequently Asked Questions DHCS
Asset limit starting July 1, 2027 $21,000 for one person, $31,000 for two, plus $1,550 per additional person (up to 10)

DHCS asset-limit FAQ. DHCS has proposed aligning the limit to the federal SSI resource limit.

 Asset Limit Frequently Asked Questions DHCS
Why this matters to you only if you have a disability An asset test plus the non-MAGI pathway is the one route out of the work rule

If you have a qualifying disability, the Aged & Disabled FPL program (138% FPL) is a non-MAGI program, and non-MAGI members are excluded from work and community engagement requirements — but your assets are then counted. With $130,000 of countable assets allowed through June 2027, most households with one home, one car and normal savings are under the limit.

 ACWDL 25-30 — Work and Community Engagement Requirements for New Adult Group DHCS

Cost sharing from October 1, 2028 (section 71120)

The same federal law that added the work requirement also changes cost sharing for expansion adults above 100% FPL, starting in 2028. It does not change your 2026 or 2027 costs, and it is a separate rule from the work requirement.

ItemValueDetailsSource
Who would pay cost sharing Expansion adults with MAGI above 100% FPL, from October 1, 2028

Section 71120 requires states to impose cost sharing greater than $0 and not more than $35 per service for ACA expansion enrollees above 100% FPL, beginning October 1, 2028, and prohibits states from imposing premiums or enrollment fees for those enrollees. Below 100% FPL this provision does not require cost sharing.

Services excluded from cost sharing Primary care, mental health, substance use disorder, FQHC, certified community behavioral health clinic, rural health clinic

The statute lists these services as excluded from the cost sharing that section 71120 requires. The aggregate out-of-pocket cap for most enrollees also continues to apply.

 CRS Report R48633 — Health Provisions in P.L. 119-21, the FY2025 Reconciliation Law Congressional Research Service (Congress.gov)
Prescription drugs Still subject to cost sharing

Prescription drugs are not on the excluded list; the specified enrollees remain subject to the prescription drug cost-sharing rules and to the aggregate out-of-pocket cap.

 CRS Report R48633 — Health Provisions in P.L. 119-21, the FY2025 Reconciliation Law Congressional Research Service (Congress.gov)
Does this affect you before 2028? No

The change begins October 1, 2028, and only reaches enrollees above 100% FPL. It does not change your 2026 or 2027 premium or cost-sharing position, and it is separate from the work requirement.

Medi-Cal groups that are not subject to the work rule

ItemValueDetailsSource
Non-MAGI Medi-Cal programs (aged, blind, disabled, long-term care, ABD FPL, Medicare Savings Programs) Excluded from the work rule

"Members who receive Medi-Cal under other coverage groups such as Non-MAGI … Mandatory Children's Group, or Pregnancy Group are excluded from work and community engagement requirements."

 ACWDL 25-30 — Work and Community Engagement Requirements for New Adult Group DHCS
Mandatory Children's Group (children) Excluded

Also excused from the six-month renewal rule.

 MEDIL I 26-25 — Six-Month Renewals and Work and Community Engagement Rules (outreach letter, incl. 'Dear Medi-Cal Member' notice dated 2026-08-31) DHCS, Medi-Cal Eligibility Division
Pregnancy Group and postpartum coverage Excluded

Pregnant individuals and those in the 12 months postpartum are excluded from both the work rule and the six-month renewal rule.

 MEDIL I 26-25 — Six-Month Renewals and Work and Community Engagement Rules (outreach letter, incl. 'Dear Medi-Cal Member' notice dated 2026-08-31) DHCS, Medi-Cal Eligibility Division
Restricted-scope (emergency) Medi-Cal and pregnancy-only services Excluded

"Individuals eligible for restricted-scope (emergency) Medi-Cal or pregnancy services are also not subject to work and community engagement requirements." Restricted-scope covers emergency care, pregnancy care and nursing home care only — it is not full coverage.

 ACWDL 25-30 — Work and Community Engagement Requirements for New Adult Group DHCS
People who move out of the MAGI New Adult Group The county stops applying the work rule

When a member moves to a coverage group that is not subject to the requirement, "the county shall stop applying the work and community engagement requirement as of the effective date of the eligibility determination." DHCS gives the example of a member whose income drop moves them to the Parent/Caretaker group.
