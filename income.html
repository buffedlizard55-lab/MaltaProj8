IRS Classification vs. Medi-Cal MAGI vs. $580 Work Test

How Rental, Interest & Kalshi Income Are Counted

Three different lenses: (1) how the IRS labels it (earned vs. unearned), (2) whether it raises your MAGI toward the Medi-Cal income limit, and (3) whether it satisfies the work-requirement income test. They are not the same question. Your three income streams all count for (2) and (3) — which is what matters.

Key distinction: The IRS calls rental, interest, and investment gains “unearned income” — but Medi-Cal and the work test still count them. “Unearned” does not mean “not counted.” It only means “not from active labor” for tax purposes.

For Your Situation — Recommendation

If you trade primarily non-sports contracts (economics, Fed rates, weather, indices, politics): capital-gains treatment is the defensible mainstream and is best for MAGI (keeps you in Medi-Cal or subsidy-eligible) and for loss netting. Document holding periods.

If you trade sports contracts: understand you carry higher gambling-classification risk and the 90% loss-cap phantom income. Keep immaculate records and consider a CPA letter supporting your filing position.

The $580 work test is independent of which tax treatment you choose. DHCS will look at countable monthly income from any source — you can document income via bank deposits + P&L even if the IRS classification is later disputed.

For MAGI: net Kalshi gain under capital treatment is what raises your MAGI. A $30k rental + $10k Kalshi net + $1k interest = $41k annual MAGI — well above Medi-Cal but squarely in Covered California subsidies. A $8k rental + $2k interest + $1k Kalshi net = $11k — Medi-Cal (pass $580 work test in months you received income).

Unearned vs. Earned — The IRS Definition

Per IRS Instructions for Form 8615 and Foreign Earned Income Exclusion page:

Earned: wages, salaries, tips, professional fees, other amounts received as pay for personal services actually performed — plus ≤30% of net business profits when capital is a material income-producing factor (e.g., property sales with personal services). · Unearned: dividends, interest, capital gains, gambling winnings, rents, royalties, pensions, annuities, alimony (pre-2019), Social Security benefits. Rental income is unearned unless personal services are performed in connection with rent production.

This classification matters for IRA contribution eligibility, EITC, and self-employment tax — not for whether you can pass the Medi-Cal $580 income test. The work test deliberately drops the earned/unearned distinction.
