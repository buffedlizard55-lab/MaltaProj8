Executive summary: your options, the exact law, and what California is doing.

This page answers four questions in order: (1) Are you in the Medi-Cal group the work rule applies to? (2) If yes, how can you satisfy it with zero earned income? (3) What are your coverage options if you are not in that group? (4) What is California actually doing to help? Every factual row links to an official source; unresolved items are flagged, not guessed.

Bottom line

You have more than one route

The strongest is your property-management work, because the federal rule expressly counts in-kind work. The simplest is $580 of MAGI-based income in one month — unearned income counts. The fallback is community service, school, a work program, or a personal exemption.

What California is not doing

California is not waiving the rule for people with zero earned income. It is implementing the federal rule and adding outreach, ex parte checks, a 30-day cure period, and a Covered California state subsidy.

The biggest risk is not the work itself. It is missing a renewal notice or failing to cure in 30 days. Keep your address current and open every county letter.

Bottom line

  1. Zero earned income is not zero income. Rental, savings interest and realized Kalshi gains are all countable income for Medi-Cal and for the $580 work-rule test. “Unearned” does not mean “ignored.”
  2. Gate 1 is the Medi-Cal income limit. For a one-person household in 2026, MAGI must be at or below $22,025/year ($1,836/month). MAGI Medi-Cal has no asset test. If you are above the limit, you are not in the group the work rule governs.
  3. Gate 2 is the community engagement rule. If you are in the group, you need one qualifying month per renewal period. The strongest route for a property manager is in-kind work: CMS expressly says a property manager or building superintendent who receives free or reduced rent can count the hours. The second route is $580 of MAGI-based income in a month; net rent, interest and realized gains count.
  4. Covered California has no work requirement. If your MAGI is between 138% and 400% FPL, you may qualify for federal help and, at or below 165% FPL in 2026 / 200% FPL in 2027, a California state subsidy. Above 400% FPL, the federal credit is unavailable for 2026.
  5. Failing the Medicaid work rule blocks marketplace subsidies. You are deemed Medicaid-eligible, so you cannot get federal premium tax credits. The fallback is cure, reapply, or pay full price.

The two gates, in order

G1

Gate 1 — Are you in the Medi-Cal MAGI New Adult Group?

Medi-Cal's MAGI New Adult Group covers adults ages 19–64 who are California residents, not pregnant, not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare, and whose household MAGI is at or below 138% of the federal poverty level. For a one-person household in 2026 that is $22,025/year or $1,836/month. There is no asset test for MAGI Medi-Cal.

If yes: Go to Gate 2. DHCS targets January 1, 2027, subject to AB 2161's systems-readiness and appropriation conditions.

If no: You are not in the MAGI New Adult Group, so the work rule does not apply to you. Covered California has no work requirement. For 2026 coverage the 400% reference for one person is $62,600; for 2027 coverage it is $63,840. Marketplace help still depends on tax-filing and other-coverage rules.

Source: DHCS eligibility chart ↗

G2

Gate 2 — Can you show one qualifying month per renewal period?

If you are in the MAGI New Adult Group and are not excluded or exempt, you must demonstrate community engagement for at least one month between eligibility determinations (the months need not be consecutive). In that month you need one of: 80 hours of work (including in-kind or unpaid work), 80 hours of community service, 80 hours in a work program, at least half-time education, a combination totaling 80 hours, or MAGI-based monthly household income of at least $580 (42 CFR 435.552(f)(2); WIC §14005.69(e)(6)). The six-month average is available only to a statutory seasonal worker (42 CFR 435.552(g)).

If yes: You keep Medi-Cal for that renewal period, provided you also complete the renewal itself on time.

If no: The county must send a notice and give you 30 calendar days to show compliance or an exemption. Coverage continues during the cure period. If you do not cure, you can be disenrolled, you are NOT eligible for federal premium tax credits while deemed Medicaid-eligible, and California lets you return without a new application during a 90-day cure period.

Source: DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗

The work rule applies only to Medi-Cal's MAGI New Adult Group. Covered California, Medicare, and community health centers do not impose it.

Your strongest routes with zero earned income

Route 1

In-kind property-management work

CMS defines work to include work in exchange for goods or services, and gives the example of a property manager or building superintendent who receives free or reduced rent. The hours spent on those duties count toward 80 per month.

Keep: the lease or rent-credit agreement, a duty list, and a monthly hours log. Ask the county what else it wants.

CMS IFR ↗

Route 2

$580 of MAGI-based income

42 CFR 435.552(f)(2) requires the state to use your MAGI-based household income for the month. Net rental income, taxable interest and realized gains count; gross receipts do not. DHCS's letter says “any source, before taxes” — that wording is broader than the regulation and is flagged.

Keep: rent deposits, interest statements, and Kalshi's P&L statement.

DHCS MEDIL I26-25 ↗

Route 3

Self-employment or fallback activity

DHCS lists self-employment as work. If owner-management is questioned, 80 hours of community service, a work program, half-time school, or a combination also satisfies the rule.

Keep: a contemporaneous log and a named contact for community service.

DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗
