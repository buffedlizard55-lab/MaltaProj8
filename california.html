What California Is Doing for Those with 0 Earned Income

California's work-rule implementation begins no sooner than January 1, 2027, subject to the enacted implementation conditions. The state specifies household MAGI-based financial methods and a seasonal-worker-only averaging alternative. It also sets verification, notice and cure protections; none establishes personal eligibility for this reader.

This is a preserved detailed page. The dated accuracy update and current Flagged for Review register take precedence over earlier simplified wording. For 2026, Medi-Cal's one-person 138% limit is $22,025/year; Covered California's 400% reference is $62,600/year (not the 2027 $63,840 reference).

California’s Mitigation — 7 Measures

1. MAGI-based income method

No wages-only gate: countable rental income and interest may enter the household calculation. The amount and tax character of Kalshi results remain unresolved; do not substitute gross receipts or payouts. (AB 2161 §14005.69(e)(6) → · DHCS MAGI chart →)

2. Seasonal-worker averaging only

The enacted six-month average requires seasonal-worker status under IRC §45R(d)(5)(B). Irregular trading alone does not show that status. (AB 2161 §14005.69(e)(7) →)

3. All hardship exemptions adopted

Disaster county + high-unemployment county auto-exempt by residence; inpatient/SNF + medical travel by request. (Implementation Plan →)

4. Ex parte first

State checks EDD/FTB/UI/TANF/SNAP/Medicare data before asking you. Only ask if data missing. (ACWDL 25-30 →)

5. Multistate legal challenge

California co-leads with Massachusetts and New Jersey in a June 29, 2026 lawsuit challenging parts of the interim rule; filing a suit does not itself suspend the requirement. (CA Attorney General →)

6. Outreach 3 months early

DHCS MEDIL I26-25 outreach letters mailed ≥3 months before first look-back, with local opportunity lists. (MEDIL I26-25 →)

7. $1.3B reflected in the enacted budget

The enacted 2026-27 Budget Act reflects about $1.3B General Fund in H.R.1-related Medi-Cal costs, plus $196.8M General Fund for county eligibility workload. The figure often quoted as “$1.1B” is not a work-rule fund; see the correction on Flagged for Review. (DHCS Budget Act highlights →)

Covered California — no Medicaid work requirement

Covered California does not impose this Medicaid work rule. Marketplace premium help depends on household MAGI, eligibility for other coverage, tax-filing rules, and the coverage year; someone disqualified from Medicaid under the work rule cannot assume federal premium tax credits. See IRS PTC eligibility and the Flagged register.
