Tax treatment of Kalshi trading gainsUnverified

Issue: The site does not state how gains and losses on Kalshi event contracts are reported for tax purposes.

What is known: KalshiEX LLC is a CFTC-designated contract market (CFTC Release 8302-20, 4 Nov 2020), and the CFTC treats the event contracts it lists as swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act. Trading is not employment, so the income is investment activity rather than earned income. No IRS guidance specific to Kalshi, or to event contracts, was located on IRS.gov during this review.

Why it matters: The exact character of the income (capital gain, other investment income, or something else) affects the MAGI figure used for Medi-Cal and Covered California, and therefore which program you land in.

What to do: Report your trading results on the application as investment income, keep your own trade records, and confirm the reporting treatment with a tax professional. Do not rely on a guess about the character of the income.

Whether your property-management work is self-employment income or rental incomeUnverified — depends on facts

Issue: The two are treated differently, and which one applies depends on facts I do not have.

What is known: HealthCare.gov lists self-employment income ("the income you expect to make from your business after expenses") and rental and royalty income ("use net rental and royalty income") as separate countable income types. IRS Publication 527 governs rental income and expenses.

Why it matters: If you are paid to manage property as a trade or business, the income is earned (self-employment) income, which is not the same as passive rent — and it would change both the MAGI figure and the work-requirement analysis.

What to do: Ask a tax professional whether your property management is a trade or business for federal tax purposes, and how it is reported. Use your answer consistently on the application and the tax return.

Three official descriptions of the $580 income test use different languageIrregularity — definitional

Issue: The statute, the county guidance, and the member notice describe the same $580 test differently.

What is known: Statute (WIC § 14005.69(e)(6)): "total monthly income, using modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)-based financial methods." County guidance (ACWDL 25-30, expressly labelled preliminary): "An individual's total gross income for a month must meet or exceed this amount," and for fluctuating income, an average of the prior six months of gross income "including earned income, unemployment benefits, State Disability Insurance, retirement income, dividends, etc." Member notice (MEDIL I 26-25): "an income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes) in any month since your last renewal."

Why it matters: Materially this rarely changes the answer for a person whose income is well above or well below $580 — and all three descriptions are satisfied by rental, interest and trading income. But if your income sits near the line, the measurement basis could matter, and the three sources do not use the same words.

What to do: Ask your county which method it applies if your monthly income is near $580.

Member notice says the rules start in March 2027; the policy letters say January 1, 2027Irregularity — date

Issue: The letter DHCS is mailing to members and the letters county staff work from give different start dates.

The high-unemployment-county hardship exemption depends on where you liveUnverified — county-specific

Issue: The exemption exists but cannot be confirmed for your county from a national source.

What is known: The exemption applies in a county with an unemployment rate at or above the lesser of 8% or 1.5 times the national unemployment rate. DHCS says it will send a separate notice if your county qualifies.

AB 2161 contains a deferral clauseWatch item

Issue: Individual pieces of California's implementation can be delayed if an appropriation is missing.

What is known: WIC § 14005.69(r): if a change creates a fiscal impact requiring an additional appropriation that has not been made, "the department may defer implementation of the change until that additional appropriation is made," and DHCS must notify the fiscal and policy committees and publish the notice on its website.

Why it matters: A deferral would change dates, not the substance of your rights — but it is the mechanism to watch for changes to the 2027 timeline.

What to do: Watch dhcs.ca.gov for any deferral notice published under this section.

California applies the work rule to state-funded adults; federal law does not require itIrregularity — policy note

Issue: The state's rule is broader than the federal minimum.

What is known: Federal law requires states to apply the community engagement requirement to federally funded adults in the ACA adult group. California law (WIC § 14005.69(b)(1)(B)) and DHCS policy (ACWDL 25-30) extend it to state-funded Medi-Cal adults meeting the same criteria, "regardless of immigration status."

Why it matters: If you are a permanent resident who has not completed the five-year bar, your Medi-Cal is state-funded, and California is the reason the work rule reaches you.

What to do: No action other than planning for the requirement. If you believe your coverage is federally funded, say so when you apply.
