Key distinction

Work hours ≠ earned income

The Medicaid rule counts work, including in-kind and unpaid work. It does not require a W-2 or a paycheck. That is why a property manager with zero earned income can still satisfy an 80-hour work test.

One month is enough

At renewal, California asks whether you met the rule in any month since the last determination. The months do not have to be consecutive. This is more forgiving than a continuous 80-hours-every-month rule.

Do not confuse the two tests. The 138% FPL test helps decide whether you qualify for MAGI Medi-Cal. The $580 income alternative is one way to meet the separate work rule if it applies. A high-income month can affect both, in opposite directions.

The cure period is the most important protection. If you receive a notice, respond within 30 days; coverage continues while you cure.

What happens if you do not comply

Two consequences, not one. After the cure period, the state may deny or disenroll you. Separately, a person who does not comply is still deemed Medicaid-eligible, which means they are not eligible for federal premium tax credits to buy a marketplace plan. You can reapply for Medicaid at any time.

Why this matters for a Kalshi trader

Trading amounts are not fixed wages; tax characterization and household MAGI are unknown. A low-income month does not itself establish the $580 alternative, and MAGI Medi-Cal has no asset test. Document a qualifying month of actual activity, an allowed MAGI-based income result, or a valid exemption for each applicable renewal period; confirm your own facts with the county.

Official quotes to check yourself

In-kind work — CMS IFR, 91 FR 33348

“There are jobs in various sectors, ranging from domestic service to specialized facility management, where individuals may choose to accept in-kind compensation in the form of non-monetary benefits like housing, meals, or utilities. For example, an individual who performs duties as a property manager or building superintendent may receive compensation in the form of free or reduced rent. Under our definition of work at § 435.552(b), the hours the individual spends performing these duties would count toward meeting the 80-hour requirement.”

CMS IFR ↗

Income from any source — DHCS MEDIL I26-25

“Have an income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes) in any month since your last renewal.”

DHCS MEDIL I26-25 ↗

Earlier preliminary total-gross wording — DHCS ACWDL 25-30

“An individual's total gross income for a month must meet or exceed this amount.”

This preliminary letter predates the enacted California income method; do not use the quotation alone to count gross rents or payouts. DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗

Enacted California household MAGI method — AB 2161 §14005.69(e)(6)–(7)

“The individual has a total monthly income, using modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)-based financial methods ... for their individual MAGI-based household budget” of at least 80 times the applicable minimum wage. The six-month alternative also requires that the individual “is a seasonal worker.”

CA AB 2161 (enacted) ↗

MAGI-based monthly household income — CMS IFR slides

“States must use an individual's modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)-based monthly household income for Medicaid to determine compliance with the community engagement requirement (i.e., generally the same income methodology used to determine financial eligibility).”

CMS IFR slides ↗
