Executive summary: your options, the exact law, and what California is doing.

This page answers four questions in order: (1) Are you in the Medi-Cal group the work rule applies to? (2) If yes, how can you satisfy it with zero earned income? (3) What are your coverage options if you are not in that group? (4) What is California actually doing to help? Bottom line

You have more than one route

The strongest is your property-management work, because the federal rule expressly counts in-kind work. The simplest is $580 of income from any source in one month. The fallback is community service, school, a work program, or a personal exemption.

What California is not doing

California is not waiving the rule for people with zero earned income. It is implementing the federal rule and adding outreach, ex parte checks, a 30-day cure period, and a Covered California state subsidy.

The biggest risk is not the work itself. It is missing a renewal notice or failing to cure in 30 days. Keep your address current and open every county letter.

Bottom line

  1. Zero earned income is not zero income. Rental, savings interest and realized Kalshi gains are all countable income for Medi-Cal and for the $580 work-rule test. “Unearned” does not mean “ignored.”
  2. Gate 1 is the Medi-Cal income limit. For a one-person household in 2026, MAGI must be at or below $22,025/year ($1,836/month). MAGI Medi-Cal has no asset test. If you are above the limit, you are not in the group the work rule governs.
  3. Gate 2 is the community engagement rule. If you are in the group, you need one qualifying month per renewal period. The strongest route for a property manager is in-kind work: CMS expressly says a property manager or building superintendent who receives free or reduced rent can count the hours. The second route is $580 of income from any source in a month.
  4. Covered California has no work requirement. If your MAGI is between 138% and 400% FPL, you may qualify for federal help and, at or below 165% FPL in 2026 / 200% FPL in 2027, a California state subsidy. Above 400% FPL, the federal credit is unavailable for 2026.
  5. Failing the Medicaid work rule blocks marketplace subsidies. You are deemed Medicaid-eligible, so you cannot get federal premium tax credits. The fallback is cure, reapply, or pay full price.

The two gates, in order

G1

Gate 1 — Are you in the Medi-Cal MAGI New Adult Group?

Medi-Cal's MAGI New Adult Group covers adults ages 19–64 who are California residents, not pregnant, not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare, and whose household MAGI is at or below 138% of the federal poverty level. For a one-person household in 2026 that is $22,025/year or $1,836/month. There is no asset test for MAGI Medi-Cal.

If yes: Go to Gate 2. The work rule applies from Jan 1, 2027 unless you are excluded or exempt.

If no: You are not eligible for MAGI Medi-Cal. Covered California has no work requirement; subsidies run from 100% to 400% FPL, with a California state subsidy up to 165% FPL in 2026 and 200% FPL in 2027.

Source: DHCS eligibility chart ↗

G2

Gate 2 — Can you show one qualifying month per renewal period?

If you are in the MAGI New Adult Group and not excluded or exempt, you must demonstrate community engagement for at least one month between eligibility determinations (the months need not be consecutive). In that month you need one of: 80 hours of work (including in-kind or unpaid work), 80 hours of community service, 80 hours in a work program, at least half-time education, a combination totaling 80 hours, or income of at least $580.

If yes: You keep Medi-Cal for that renewal period, provided you also complete the renewal itself on time.

If no: The county must send a notice and give you 30 calendar days to show compliance or an exemption. Coverage continues during the cure period. If you do not cure, you can be disenrolled and you are NOT eligible for federal premium tax credits while deemed Medicaid-eligible.

Source: DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗

Your strongest routes with zero earned income

Route 1

In-kind property-management work

CMS defines work to include work in exchange for goods or services, and gives the example of a property manager or building superintendent who receives free or reduced rent. The hours spent on those duties count toward 80 per month.

Keep: the lease or rent-credit agreement, a duty list, and a monthly hours log. Ask the county what else it wants.

CMS IFR ↗

Route 2

$580 of income from any source

DHCS's member letter says “income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes) in any month since your last renewal.” Rental, interest and realized Kalshi gains can count. One month is enough.

Keep: rent deposits, interest statements, and Kalshi's P&L statement.

DHCS MEDIL I26-25 ↗

Route 3

Self-employment or fallback activity

DHCS lists self-employment as work. If owner-management is questioned, 80 hours of community service, a work program, half-time school, or a combination also satisfies the rule.

Keep: a contemporaneous log and a named contact for community service.

DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗
