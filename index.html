You can satisfy the work rule with $0 earned income — if you document the right route.

Short answer: you do not need a W-2 to satisfy California's Medicaid work rule. There are three independent routes: count your property-management hours (the federal rule expressly counts in-kind work, including free or reduced rent), show $580 of income from any source in one month, or qualify for an exclusion or exemption. If your MAGI is above the Medi-Cal limit, Covered California has no work requirement at all. Every row below links to an official source.

The two gates

First: are you in the Medi-Cal MAGI New Adult Group (income ≤ 138% FPL)? Second: can you show one qualifying month per renewal period? Most of the confusion disappears once you separate the two.

Read the executive summary →

The strongest route for a property manager

CMS's rule defines work to include in-kind work and gives the example of a property manager or building superintendent who receives free or reduced rent. Those hours count toward 80/month.

42 CFR 435.552(b) · 91 FR 33348

The trap: if you fail the work rule, you are still deemed Medicaid-eligible and you are not eligible for federal premium tax credits. The fallback is not automatically Covered California with a subsidy.

Your answer in 30 seconds

Route 1 — strongest

In-kind property-management work

If you manage the building where you live, or receive free or reduced rent for management duties, the federal rule says those hours count as work. Aim for 80 hours in at least one month per renewal period. Keep a duty list, the rent-credit agreement, and a monthly log.

CMS IFR ↗

Route 2 — simplest

$580 of income from any source

DHCS's member letter says “income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes) in any month since your last renewal.” Rental, interest and realized Kalshi gains can all count. One month is enough.

DHCS MEDIL I26-25 ↗

Route 3 — fallback

Exclusion, exemption, or 80 hours of activity

If neither route fits, the rule can be met with 80 hours of community service, a work program, at least half-time school, or a combination — or you may be excluded or exempt (age, disability, caregiving, Medicare, high-unemployment county).

DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗

But first check Gate 1. Medi-Cal's MAGI New Adult Group is limited to household income at or below 138% FPL — $22,025/year or $1,836/month for one person in 2026. If your rental + interest + Kalshi MAGI is above that, you are not in this program and the work rule does not apply to you; Covered California has no work requirement. The site cannot compute your MAGI because you did not supply the amounts. See Coverage Options.

The two gates — do them in order

G1

Gate 1 — Are you in the Medi-Cal MAGI New Adult Group?

Medi-Cal's MAGI New Adult Group covers adults ages 19–64 who are California residents, not pregnant, not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare, and whose household MAGI is at or below 138% of the federal poverty level. For a one-person household in 2026 that is $22,025/year or $1,836/month. There is no asset test for MAGI Medi-Cal.

If yes: Go to Gate 2. The work rule applies from Jan 1, 2027 unless you are excluded or exempt.

If no: You are not eligible for MAGI Medi-Cal. Covered California has no work requirement; subsidies run from 100% to 400% FPL, with a California state subsidy up to 165% FPL in 2026 and 200% FPL in 2027.

Source: DHCS eligibility chart ↗

G2

Gate 2 — Can you show one qualifying month per renewal period?

If you are in the MAGI New Adult Group and not excluded or exempt, you must demonstrate community engagement for at least one month between eligibility determinations (the months need not be consecutive). In that month you need one of: 80 hours of work (including in-kind or unpaid work), 80 hours of community service, 80 hours in a work program, at least half-time education, a combination totaling 80 hours, or income of at least $580.

If yes: You keep Medi-Cal for that renewal period, provided you also complete the renewal itself on time.

If no: The county must send a notice and give you 30 calendar days to show compliance or an exemption. Coverage continues during the cure period. If you do not cure, you can be disenrolled and you are NOT eligible for federal premium tax credits while deemed Medicaid-eligible.

Source: DHCS ACWDL 25-30 ↗

Both gates must be true before the work rule applies. Gate 1 is an income test; Gate 2 is a work/income/exemption test.
