The averaging trap

If you have $0 earned income and irregular trading gains, the single most dangerous sentence on this site used to be that you can average six months of income to reach $580. Federally you cannot, unless you are a seasonal worker. Rely on one month at $580 instead.

The averaging path is narrower than California’s letter

42 CFR 435.552(g)(1) allows a six-month average only where the individual “is a seasonal worker as described in 26 U.S.C. 45R(d)(5)(B)”. That definition covers labour or services performed on a seasonal basis as defined by the Secretary of Labor — in practice seasonal agricultural workers and retail workers employed exclusively during holiday seasons. Own-account trading is neither. The CMS fact sheet agrees: “$580 per month in 2026; for seasonal workers, there is a different calculation.” DHCS’s member letter is broader (“people whose hours change frequently or who have seasonal jobs”), and that divergence is flagged rather than resolved.

42 CFR 435.552(g)(1) · CMS fact sheet · DHCS MEDIL I 26-25

“Gross income” is not the federal measure

42 CFR 435.552(f)(2) requires the agency to determine monthly income “based on the individual’s MAGI-based income, for their MAGI-based household, as defined at § 435.603”. MAGI starts from adjusted gross income. For a property owner that means net rent after expenses, not gross receipts — so the older “gross income” wording could make someone believe they clear $580 when they do not. DHCS ACWDL 25-30 does say “total gross income”, but it is dated December 30 2025 and states on its face that it is preliminary guidance subject to change; the codified rule took effect July 31 2026.
