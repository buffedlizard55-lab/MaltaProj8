The new work requirement, in detail

Section 71119 of H.R. 1 (Public Law 119-21) and California's implementation of it: who is subject, how to comply, who is exempt, and what happens if you do not comply.

The new law, exactly

The requirement is federal law (Public Law 119-21, § 71119) and it is already written into California statute (Welfare and Institutions Code § 14005.69, as amended by AB 2161). Both texts are linked below.

How to comply in a month

Any one of these is enough for the month. They can also be combined.

Work 80 hours or more in the month

California's law: "The individual works no less than 80 hours." DHCS adds that a job includes self-employment, working at least 80 hours a month.

Income test — the practical route for a trader Total monthly income of at least $580 (80 × $7.25 federal minimum wage)

California law (WIC § 14005.69(e)(6)) measures this as "total monthly income, using modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)-based financial methods." DHCS policy guidance describes it as "An individual's total gross income for a month must meet or exceed this amount." The member notice says "an income from any source of at least $580 (before taxes) in any month since your last renewal." For fluctuating income, DHCS allows averaging the prior 6 months; the member notice says the same: "People whose hours change frequently or who have seasonal jobs can average at least $580 per month from any source over 6 months."

Seasonal worker is defined by section 45R(d)(5)(B) of the Internal Revenue Code. In California this is WIC § 14005.69(e)(7).

California's one extension of the federal rule

Federal law requires states to apply the community engagement requirement to federally funded adults in the ACA adult group (§ 1902(a)(10)(A)(i)(VIII)). California went further and included state-funded Medi-Cal adults who meet the same criteria (AB 2161 § 14005.69(b)(1)(B)), and DHCS policy states the requirement applies "regardless of immigration status." If you are a permanent resident who has completed the five-year bar you are federally funded and this makes no difference; if you are still inside the five-year bar your coverage is state-funded, and California will still apply the work rule to you from 2027.
